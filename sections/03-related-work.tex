\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

Prior work bears on \crystal along four lines: the requirements and
surveys of immersive communication, systems and transports for
volumetric streaming, subjective studies of point-cloud video, and
parametric \qoe models. Table~\ref{tab:positioning} positions this
article against the closest studies on the axes that define its
contribution: whether a \qoe model is fitted to subjective scores,
whether network delay and loss are the independent variables, whether
the transport is unmodified reliable \quic, and whether the model
offers a mechanism rather than a fit.

\subsection{Immersive Media Streaming for \gls{vr}}
\label{sec:rw-immersive}

Surveys of immersive media delivery agree on the gap this article
addresses while leaving it open. Van der Hooft et al.~\cite{Immersivevideo}
and Viola and Cesar~\cite{Viola2023} give the end-to-end tutorial view
of volumetric delivery, from capture to \gls{6dof} rendering in the
\gls{hmd}, and identify \gls{qoe} modelling under network impairment as
unresolved. Shen et al.~\cite{shen2023toward} argue that packet loss,
delay variation and bandwidth limits produce cascading effects on
immersive \gls{qoe} that 2D video metrics cannot capture, and call for
models that connect network conditions to perception. Garcia et
al.~\cite{garcia22} classify volumetric telepresence under ``Meet'' and
``Visit'' archetypes and find that published evaluations combine
presence questionnaires and technical metrics without a standard
framework. Liu et al.~\cite{pcstreaming21} and Enenche et
al.~\cite{metaverseroad25} survey point-cloud streaming and state
plainly that measuring its \gls{qoe} ``is still in its infancy''. None
of these supplies a model that predicts a subjective score from
network conditions for a specific codec and transport.

\subsection{\vpcc Streaming Systems and Transport}
\label{sec:rw-systems}

Systems work on \vpcc delivery attacks bandwidth and adaptation and
evaluates objectively. Rudolph et al.~\cite{rudolph2025rabbit}
transcode \vpcc streams in real time by re-encoding only the
underlying video sub-streams, evaluated through rate--distortion and
transcoding latency; QV4~\cite{qv424} adds viewpoint-aware tiling to
\vpcc by exploiting the encoder's intermediate outputs, with a \qoe
model trained on VOLVQAD ratings and a \gls{6dof} movement model, evaluated
by simulation on bandwidth adaptation rather than delay or loss.
Codec-agnostic systems follow the same pattern: Hermes~\cite{hermes}
exploits implicit inter-frame correlation on commodity phones over LTE
and Wi-Fi traces; YuZu~\cite{yuzu} applies 3D super-resolution and
validates with more than 1,400 remote participants on monitors; P2VS~\cite{p2vs}
achieves codec-independent progressive streaming in simulation; De
Fré et al.~\cite{mathias24} deliver one-to-many \gls{webrtc} conferencing
with multiple-description coding, reporting \gls{vmaf}; Suthar et
al.~\cite{codec} switch between codecs for rate adaptation on
simulated traces. In all of these the encoder configuration, in
particular the \gof length, is held fixed, and none exposes the
content to a real transport under controlled impairment with
subjective validation in a head-mounted display.

On transport, Ravuri et al.~\cite{ravuri2023} integrate \gls{dash} with
partially reliable \quic for \vpcc, deciding per object whether to
deliver reliably, and show 46\% higher throughput and 33\% fewer
playout interruptions than reliable \quic at 5\% Gilbert--Elliott
loss on 5G traces, with a headless player. Octavius~\cite{haseeb2025}
combines adaptive encoding with a mixed-reliability \quic protocol;
Freeman et al.~\cite{moq2025} obtain implicit server-side adaptation
from delivery timeouts in Media-over-\quic; Thomas and
Xylomenos~\cite{thomas2025} demonstrate ultra-low-latency point-cloud
streaming on a real 5G testbed with forward error correction but
without \quic; Fiorina et al.~\cite{fiorina2025} estimate \qoe for
multicast point-cloud streaming over 5G from an objective model only.
Each either modifies the fully reliable protocol that production
stacks ship~\cite{rfc9000} or omits subjective evaluation. That
reliable \quic turns loss into delay, and delay into \gof stalls, is
noted qualitatively in several of these works, but its perceptual cost
has not been quantified. Hong et al.~\cite{hong2026latency} decompose
the end-to-end latency of a real-time volumetric pipeline across
application, transport and network layers, an objective complement to
the subjective calibration here. To the best of our knowledge, before
\cite{mosaic2026} no study combined real \vpcc transmission over
\quic, playback in a \gls{vr} headset, controlled impairment injection on a
real link, 5G delivery and a standardised subjective study; no study
yet combines those with a model.

\subsection{Subjective Quality of Point-Cloud Video}
\label{sec:rw-subjective}

Subjective studies of volumetric video are dominated by compression
and bandwidth. Van der Hooft et al.~\cite{vanderhooft2020qomex} run the
first subjective evaluation of adaptive \vpcc streaming, with 30
participants rating pre-rendered viewports on a flat screen under
bandwidth and buffer variation at a fixed \SI{37}{\milli\second} latency.
Subramanyam et al.~\cite{subramanyam2022} evaluate tiled user-adaptive
real-time point-cloud streaming for \gls{vr} remote communication with
33 users in head-mounted displays, varying bandwidth only. Zhou et
al.~\cite{zhou2025tomm} collect quality scores and eye-tracking
attention maps from 40 participants viewing \vpcc, \gpcc and
CWI-PCL-coded dynamic point clouds with \gls{6dof} in \gls{vr}, without
a network in the loop. Cox et al.~\cite{Cox2023} release VOLVQAD, 7,680 ratings on 376 \vpcc
sequences from 120 participants under the Degradation Category Rating
method, finding texture compression more damaging than geometry and
documenting the effect of quality-switching patterns; the study used
2D displays and no network impairment. Nguyen et al.~\cite{nguyen2023}
characterise \qoe and immersion of \vpcc point clouds in augmented
reality on the HoloLens~2 with 32 participants across quantisation,
quality switches, viewing distance and content, without a stated
transport or network variable. Alexiou et al.~\cite{Alexiou2023} survey
the methodology; Ak et al.~\cite{ak2024basics} benchmark objective
metrics on a broad static corpus, where \pcqm~\cite{pcqm} is among the
most stable across codecs. Liu et al.~\cite{liu2021tip} fit a
reduced-reference perceptual model to \vpcc quantisation parameters.

Network impairment as the independent variable is rare, and where it
appears it is applied at the wrong layer for the question asked here.
Dumic and da Silva Cruz~\cite{dumic2023} inject
\SIrange{0.5}{2}{\percent} packet loss into \vpcc sub-bitstreams in
MATLAB before decoding and report MOS drops exceeding one scale unit
at \SI{0.5}{\percent}, with geometry and occupancy losses far more
damaging than attribute losses; the content is shown as 2D projected
video on a conventional display. Wu et al.~\cite{wu2021nossdav} study
distortion and concealment for dynamic point clouds under loss. Both
remove packets from the bitstream and ask the decoder to cope. Under
reliable \quic the decoder never sees an incomplete bitstream; the
impairment is temporal, and neither study captures the retransmission
dynamics that real loss elicits nor the stereoscopic, head-tracked
viewing that defines immersive \gls{vr}. The only subjective study of
volumetric media under delay \emph{and} loss on reliable transport in
head-mounted displays, prior to~\cite{mosaic2026}, is that of Udora et
al., discussed next.

\subsection{Parametric \qoe Models for Volumetric Media}
\label{sec:rw-models}

The exponential form of \crystal's perceptual layer descends from the
IQX hypothesis of Fiedler et al.~\cite{fiedler2010}, which derives
$\mathrm{QoE} = a\,e^{-b x} + c$ for a single impairment $x$ from the
assumption that the sensitivity of \qoe to an impairment is
proportional to the current \qoe level; Reichl et al.~\cite{reichl2010}
argue for a logarithmic law under the Weber--Fechner interpretation, and
the two agree in the operating region relevant here. Both are
single-impairment, content-agnostic descriptions of a QoS--\qoe
relationship~\cite{qoemodels}.

For volumetric media, three families of model exist. The first embeds
a \qoe formula inside an optimiser. Wen et al.~\cite{wen2024} define
$\mathrm{QoE}_k(t) = Q_k(t) - \alpha_1\,\Delta Q_k(t) - \alpha_2\,B_k(t)$,
a weighted sum of instantaneous quality, quality fluctuation and
rebuffering, as the reward of a proximal-policy-optimisation controller
for cooperative volumetric streaming; QV4~\cite{qv424} and Wang et
al.~\cite{wang2022tmm} follow the same pattern with bitrate/stall
utilities. Li et al.~\cite{li2021qoe} are the exception that fits a
model to head-mounted-display ratings: from 34 participants viewing
\vpcc content on an HTC Vive under simulated bandwidth, they fit a
linear regression on objective quality, stall time and quality
switches. Stalls enter as a measured outcome of bandwidth starvation,
not as a function of delay, loss and \gof structure, so the model
cannot be evaluated at a new operating point without re-running the
experiment. These are evaluated in simulation, specify no transport, and
are calibrated on 2D-video conventions rather than volumetric
subjective data. The second family maps features to quality without a
network variable: Van Damme et al.~\cite{vandamme2022,vandamme2024}
build a clustering-based psychometric no-reference model for
\vpcc video, a sigmoidal mapping of image features trained against
\gls{vmaf} at video, \gof and frame granularity, on an emulated network with
latency fixed at \SI{37}{\milli\second}; Zhang et al.~\cite{zhang2019towards}
use a Mamdani fuzzy inference system over subjectively rated content,
hardware, environment and interaction factors for the HoloLens.

The third family, and the direct baseline for this work, is the
network-aware regression of Udora et al.~\cite{qoeholographic,
udora2024quality}, developed in the SPIRIT project for live volumetric
telepresence and extended in Udora's doctoral
thesis~\cite{udora2025thesis}. From a double-stimulus study with 25
participants in head-mounted displays, they fit
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{QoE} = a\,e^{b D} + c\,\plr + d,
  \label{eq:udora}
\end{equation}
with coefficients $(a,b,c,d)$ re-estimated for each of three point-cloud
density levels, producing a MOS on the 1--5 scale. Three features of
this work make it the right comparator. Its transport is reliable
(\gls{tcp}) so its data carry the same loss-to-latency signature as ours;
\quic is listed as future work. Its functional form is additive in
delay and loss, which is exactly the separability that \crystal predicts will fail. And its held-out performance is modest, with \gls{plcc} between 0.57 and
0.66 across quality levels, which is what one expects when five free
coefficients absorb a mechanism rather than express it. Udora's
uncompressed per-frame streaming also provides an external reference
point for the case of no \gof coupling, where a lost packet delays only
one frame.

\crystal differs on each count. It replaces the five coefficients with
one perceptual parameter and a measured ceiling; it derives the
explanatory variable $\tlf$ from transport quantities instead of
fitting delay and loss directly; it treats separability as a hypothesis
to be tested and finds it false; and it makes \gof size, not codec
identity or quality level, the structural variable. Beyond these
specific models, there is no standardised \qoe metric for volumetric
content; most proposals adapt 2D metrics or rely on raw subjective
scores, and their computational cost varies widely, which limits
real-time deployment~\cite{Immersivevideo}.

\subsection{Gaussian Splatting}
\label{sec:rw-3dgs}

3D Gaussian Splatting~\cite{kerbl2023gaussian} has acquired a streaming
literature of its own: layered \gls{dash} delivery~\cite{sun2025lts},
in-network assistance~\cite{krishna2025netsplat}, rate adaptation over
dynamic networks~\cite{wang2026tcsvt}, motion-residual
prediction~\cite{naqvi2026cubegs} and fine-granular scalable
bitstreams~\cite{talha2026splatstream}. All evaluate with objective
rendering metrics; no subjective study of splat streaming under network
impairment exists. Because each Gaussian primitive is independently
decodable, a splat stream has no \gof coupling in the sense of
Section~\ref{sec:bg-codecs}, and \crystal's Layer~1 makes a specific
prediction about its resilience (Section~\ref{sec:model-predict}).
That prediction is left for future work but stated so that the model
can be refuted by it.
