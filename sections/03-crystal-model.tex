\section{The \crystal Model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Scope and Rated Attribute}
\label{sec:model-scope}
\crystal models the \emph{network-attributable} component of \qoe for
\vpcc streaming over reliable \quic: the degradation in perceived
temporal smoothness caused by delay and loss, relative to an
impairment-free presentation of the same content. It does not model
compression fidelity, which is fixed per sequence and reported through
\pcqm as a stimulus descriptor, and it does not model overall MOS as a
combination of fidelity and smoothness. The rated attribute in the
subjective study is smoothness of playback.

\subsection{Delivery Unit and Loss Recovery}
\label{sec:model-unit}
Two properties of the stream and the transport determine how a network
impairment reaches the viewer. The first is the delivery unit. \vpcc,
the principal component of ISO/IEC 23090-5~\cite{v3c,vpccgraziosi},
projects a dynamic point cloud into geometry, attribute and occupancy
atlases coded as ordinary video with \gls{hevc}~\cite{hevc,tmc2}, and so
inherits the video codec's prediction structure: frames are coded in
\glspl{gof}, either \gls{ra} with I-, P- and B-frames and a \gof of 32,
or \gls{ldp} with I- and P-frames and \gofs of 16 or
4~\cite{vpccctc,hmctc}. A \gof must be received in full before any of
its frames can be reconstructed, and in a streaming deployment it
carries a presentation deadline. The \gof is therefore the
\emph{delivery unit}, and its size is the structural variable of the
model: a smaller \gof puts fewer packets at risk but leaves a tighter
deadline budget.

The second is loss recovery. Each \gof is fetched as one HTTP object
over \gls{http3}~\cite{rfc9114}, so every packet of a unit travels on
one \quic stream~\cite{rfc9000}. Standard \quic never reuses packet
numbers and declares a packet lost either when one at least
$k_{\mathrm{PacketThreshold}}=3$ later is acknowledged or, if no later
acknowledgement arrives, when a \gls{pto} expires~\cite{rfc9002}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{PTO} = \mathrm{sRTT} + \max(4\,\mathrm{RTT}_{\mathrm{var}},
  k_{\mathrm{granularity}}) + \mathrm{max\_ack\_delay}.
  \label{eq:pto}
\end{equation}
A loss therefore costs at least one round trip, and a full \gls{pto}
when the lost packet is among the last of the unit, and each recovery
is followed by a congestion-window reduction that persists into later
transfers. Because \quic is reliable, the decoder never receives an
incomplete \gof: loss becomes \emph{delay}, and delay becomes a
\emph{stall} when the unit misses its deadline. Our measurements show
the two impairments leave distinct signatures, added latency shifting
the delivery-time distribution and loss expanding its
tail~\cite{vquest2026}, and that the per-\gof deadline-miss rate
correlates only partially with subjective quality ($\rho = -0.34$)
because a missed \gof of 4 frames and one of 16 frames remove different
durations of content~\cite{mosaic2026}. The temporal loss fraction
defined next closes that gap.

\subsection{Layer 1: Transport Model}
\label{sec:model-layer1}

Layer~1 maps the network condition and the encoder configuration to a
single observable, the \emph{temporal loss fraction} $\tlf$, the share
of wall-clock playback time during which the renderer is not showing
the frame it should. It contains no perceptual quantity: every term is
either a transport parameter or a property of the bitstream, and every
intermediate is measurable from the renderer's telemetry.

\paragraph{Delivery unit and deadline.}
The stream is a sequence of delivery units $k = 1, 2, \ldots$; for
\vpcc the delivery unit is the \gof, with $G$ frames at frame rate $f$
and nominal duration $T_u = G/f$. Unit $k$ has $n_k = \lceil B_k / P
\rceil$ packets, where $B_k$ is its encoded size and $P$ the payload per
\quic packet (\SI{1376}{B} on the testbed, Section~\ref{sec:system}).
The client fetches units over a persistent \gls{http3} connection with
a paced controller that keeps up to $W$ units in flight and starts
unit $k$ at $r_k$; the unit must be decoded by its presentation
deadline $d_k$. Under no impairment, delivery takes $D^0_k = \rho +
B_k/C$, one round trip $\rho$ for the request plus transfer at goodput
$C$, and the \emph{slack}
\begin{equation}
  S_k = d_k - r_k - D^0_k
  \label{eq:slack}
\end{equation}
is the budget the unit has for absorbing a disturbance. $S_k$ is set by
the prefetch margin and the pacing of the controller, grows with $T_u$,
and is read directly from Slice~A telemetry rather than modelled.

\paragraph{How delay and loss consume slack.}
Added one-way latency $\ell$ raises the round trip to $\rho' = \rho +
2\ell$ and so adds $2\ell$ to every unit's delivery time; it also
lowers the goodput a window-limited transfer can reach, which we fold
into $C(\rho')$. Packet loss at rate $p$ never removes data from the
stream: a unit with at least one lost packet, which happens with
probability $1 - (1-p)^{n_k} \approx n_k p$, is completed only after
recovery. Each loss event costs a recovery time $R$ that is at least
one round trip when later packets keep flowing and a full \gls{pto}
when the lost packet is among the last of the unit (a tail loss),
\begin{equation}
  R(\rho') = \rho' + \pi_{\mathrm{tail}}\,[\mathrm{PTO}(\rho') - \rho'],
  \qquad \pi_{\mathrm{tail}} \approx \frac{k_{\mathrm{PacketThreshold}}}{n_k},
  \label{eq:recovery}
\end{equation}
with $\mathrm{PTO}$ from Eq.~\eqref{eq:pto}; the accompanying
congestion-window reduction lowers $C$ for the following units and is
absorbed into $C(\rho')$ as a measured, not fitted, quantity. With
$L_k \sim \mathrm{Binomial}(n_k, p)$ loss events, the delivery time of
unit $k$ under impairment is
\begin{equation}
  D_k = 2\ell + \frac{B_k}{C(\rho')} - \frac{B_k}{C} + L_k\, R(\rho'),
  \label{eq:delay}
\end{equation}
and the unit misses its deadline when $D_k > S_k$.

\paragraph{From misses to temporal loss.}
The renderer's policy on a miss is the one of~\cite{mosaic2026}: the
late unit is dropped and the last good unit is held, so a miss costs
the viewer the $G$ frames of that unit, shown as a freeze of length
$T_u$ followed by a skip. A unit that arrives late but within the
hold window contributes a freeze of length $\tau_k = \max(0, D_k -
S_k)$. Over a session of $N$ units the temporal loss fraction is
\begin{equation}
  \tlf = \frac{1}{N\,T_u}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\Big[\tau_k + T_u\,\mathbb{1}(D_k - S_k > H)\Big]
  \;=\; \crystallayerone,
  \label{eq:tlf}
\end{equation}
where $H$ is the hold window after which the unit is dropped, and the
second form is the operational definition measured from telemetry: one
minus the ratio of frames rendered on schedule to frames due. Taking
expectations over $L_k$ gives $\tlf$ as a function of
$(\ell, p, G, B_k, S_k, \rho, C)$ with no free parameter; the
$B_k$, $S_k$, $\rho$ and $C$ are measured on the clean slice and the
rest are experimental settings.

\paragraph{Two consequences.}
Equation~\eqref{eq:delay} contains the model's two structural claims.
First, each impairment degrades $\tlf$ on its own, and the two do not
add. For a single unit a miss is a threshold event in the combined
load $2\ell + L_k R$ against the slack $S_k$; across a session the
slack, size and loss count vary from unit to unit, so the
session-level $\tlf$ is a smooth, convex function of the load: added
latency alone pushes the units with the least slack over their
deadlines, loss alone does the same for the units that draw the most
recovery, and when both are present every unit faces the sum of the
two, so the units that each impairment alone would have spared are
now lost too. The model therefore predicts graded single-factor
effects and a super-additive combination: $\mos_A > \mos_B$,
$\mos_A > \mos_C$, and $(\mos_A - \mos_D) > (\mos_A - \mos_B) +
(\mos_A - \mos_C)$. An additive form such as Eq.~\eqref{eq:udora}
cannot produce the last inequality.
Second, the \gof size $G$ enters on both sides of the inequality. A
larger unit has more packets at risk ($n_k \propto G$) and so more loss
events per unit, but it also has proportionally more slack ($S_k$
grows with $T_u = G/f$) and amortises the fixed per-request cost
$2\ell$ over $G$ frames; the per-frame cost of latency falls as $1/G$
while the per-frame cost of loss is roughly constant. For the
loss-dominated and latency-dominated regimes of the factorial design
this predicts that \gls{ldp} \gof{}=16 is \emph{more} resilient than
\gof{}=4 at matched impairment, the opposite of the intuition that
shorter units limit damage, and the opposite of what loss magnitude
alone would predict.

\subsection{Layer 2: Perceptual Model}
\label{sec:model-layer2}

Layer~2 maps $\tlf$ to the mean opinion score of the rated attribute,
playback smoothness. We adopt the IQX relation of Fiedler et
al.~\cite{fiedler2010}: the sensitivity of perceived quality to an
additional unit of impairment is proportional to the quality that
remains, $\mathrm{d}Q/\mathrm{d}\tlf = -\alpha\,(Q - Q_{\min})$, whose
solution on the five-point \gls{acr} scale with floor $Q_{\min} = 1$ is
\begin{equation}
  \crystalmodel,
  \label{eq:crystal}
\end{equation}
where $\qc \in (0, 1]$ is the clean-reference ceiling of content $c$
and $\alpha > 0$ is the single perceptual parameter. The ceiling is
not fitted: it is anchored to the clean slice,
\begin{equation}
  \qc = \frac{\mosm_{A,c} - 1}{4},
  \label{eq:ceiling}
\end{equation}
so that the model reproduces the impairment-free rating of each
content by construction and $\alpha$ carries only the response to
temporal loss. The model has the boundary behaviour required of a
smoothness score, $\widehat{\mosm} \to 1 + 4\qc$ as $\tlf \to 0$ and
$\widehat{\mosm} \to 1$ as $\tlf \to \infty$, and $\alpha$ has a direct
reading: $\tlf_{1/2} = \ln 2/\alpha$ is the temporal loss at which the
rating has lost half its headroom above the floor.

Two design decisions follow from the rated attribute. Objective
fidelity does not enter Eq.~\eqref{eq:crystal}: \pcqm is reported per
sequence as a stimulus descriptor (Section~\ref{sec:sys-content}), but
spatial fidelity is fixed within a session and has no bearing on
perceived smoothness, so giving it a coefficient would only absorb
variance that belongs to content. And because the clean slice is
rendered without stalls, the model predicts that the three $\qc$ are
close to one and statistically indistinguishable, in which case
Eq.~\eqref{eq:crystal} collapses to $\widehat{\mosm} = 1 + 4e^{-\alpha\tlf}$,
a one-parameter curve shared by all content.

The two layers are falsifiable separately. Layer~1 predicts a measured
quantity, $\tlf$, from settings and clean-slice telemetry, and can be
checked against the renderer's deadline log without any rating.
Layer~2 is fitted on measured $\tlf$, not on modelled $\tlf$, so an
error in Layer~1 cannot be hidden by $\alpha$; conversely, a good
Layer~2 fit on measured $\tlf$ with a poor Layer~1 prediction would
locate the failure in the transport model alone. Compared with
Eq.~\eqref{eq:udora}, which fits delay and loss directly with five
coefficients per quality level, \crystal moves the explanatory burden
into a derived variable and leaves one constant to be learned from
people.

\subsection{Model Predictions}
\label{sec:model-predictions}
Because $\tlf$ is derived rather than fitted, the model commits to
three predictions before any rating is collected, and the evaluation of
Section~\ref{sec:results} is organised around them.

\emph{Latency and loss each degrade quality, and together they
degrade it more than additively.} Both impairments consume the same
per-unit slack in Eq.~\eqref{eq:delay}, so each alone produces a
measurable temporal loss and a \mos drop, and their combination
produces a drop larger than the sum of the two, which an additive form
such as Eq.~\eqref{eq:udora} cannot reproduce. Across the four slices
of Section~\ref{sec:design-network} the model predicts
$\mos_A > \mos_B,\, \mos_C > \mos_D$ with
$(\mos_A - \mos_D) > (\mos_A - \mos_B) + (\mos_A - \mos_C)$.

\emph{Delivery-unit structure, not loss magnitude, governs
resilience.} For fixed delay and loss, $\tlf$ depends on the \gof size
$G$ through both the number of packets at risk per unit and the slack
available to recover them, and Layer~1 predicts that \gls{ldp}
\gof{}=16 is more resilient than \gof{}=4 at matched impairment.

\emph{One perceptual parameter generalises.} A single $\alpha$ fitted
on one subset of conditions predicts held-out conditions without
refitting, at a held-out \gls{plcc} exceeding the 0.57--0.66 reported
for the five-coefficient regression of Eq.~\eqref{eq:udora}.
