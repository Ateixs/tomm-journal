\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Immersive applications increasingly define the trajectory from \fiveg
to \gls{6g}~\cite{shen2023toward}: 3GPP lists immersive and
extended-reality services among the primary usage scenarios of these
generations~\cite{3gpp22261}, and mobile
video traffic is projected to more than double by
2031~\cite{mobility25nov}. The most demanding of these services is
immersive media streaming for \gls{vr}: volumetric content captured from
a real scene and reproduced in an \gls{hmd} with
\gls{6dof}~\cite{Immersivevideo,Viola2023}. Its dominant format, the
dynamic point cloud, requires gigabits per second
uncompressed~\cite{pcstreaming21}, and \gls{mpeg}'s
\gls{vpcc}~\cite{v3c,vpccgraziosi} is the reference codec for dense,
dynamic content. \vpcc codes geometry, attribute and occupancy atlases
with \gls{hevc}~\cite{hevc} and inherits its temporal prediction
structure: the bitstream is organized in \glspl{gof}, each of which must
be received in full before any of its frames can be decoded. Because
standalone \glspl{hmd} cannot decode \vpcc in real time, deployments
render at an edge server and cast the frames to the headset, so the
path from the content origin to the edge renderer is where network
impairment reaches the content.

Volumetric streaming research has largely assumed lossy datagram
transport or partially reliable \quic variants that drop stale
data~\cite{ravuri2023,haseeb2025}. Production systems run neither: the
\quic shipped in browsers, content delivery networks and mobile
operating systems is fully reliable~\cite{rfc9000}. No bitstream reaches
the decoder incomplete; loss becomes delay through retransmission and
congestion-window reduction, and the \gof amplifies that delay, since
one late packet stalls the whole unit. The perceptual consequence is
temporal rather than geometric: freezes and frame skips, not holes in
the surface. Our earlier work showed that latency and loss leave
distinct transport signatures~\cite{vquest2026}, and that a \vpcc slice
with \emph{lower} loss but a tighter \gof deadline can score lower
than one with higher loss~\cite{mosaic2026}. Existing \qoe models do not
capture this regime. The closest, by Udora et
al.~\cite{qoeholographic,udora2026tmm}, fits an exponential-in-delay,
linear-in-loss regression per quality level and reaches held-out
correlations of 0.57--0.66; it describes its data but not \emph{why}
delay and loss degrade \qoe, and so cannot transfer to a new \gof size,
encoder or network.

We present \crystal, a mechanistic two-layer \qoe model for \vpcc
streaming over reliable \quic. Layer~1, which has no perceptual
parameter, explains how delay and loss turn into skipped delivery
units, through a per-unit budget set by the \gof period, a slack that
delay shrinks and each loss spends, and a decoder buffer that drains
when slow units run in sequence; its observable is the \emph{skip
fraction} $\tlf$, read from the client's session counters. Layer~2
maps $\tlf$ to opinion score with one constant,

where $\qc$ is the clean-reference ceiling of each content and $\alpha$
is the only fitted parameter. The model claims that, under reliable
transport, latency and loss act on perceived smoothness only through
presentation timing. We test it on \mosaic~\cite{mosaic2026}, an
end-to-end pipeline that streams \vpcc over \gls{http3} to an edge
renderer colocated with a private \fiveg network and casts the
rendered frames to a Meta Quest~3, with an ITU-T P.910 \gls{acr} study
($N=50$) over four slices at a fixed encoder configuration: a clean
reference, loss only, delay only, and a combination of a delay and a
loss level that are each harmless alone. The contributions are:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,nosep]
  \item[--] \textbf{An end-to-end pipeline for \vpcc over reliable \quic
        to a \gls{vr} headset}, documented in full: an \gls{hm}-compatible
        x265 shim for tractable \gls{tmc2} encoding; a native lsquic
        \gls{http3} client with per-\gof cancellation and a deadline
        scheduler on a content clock, feeding the 5G-MAG decoder through a
        drop-in replacement of its \gls{dash} client; a fix for a decoder
        teardown deadlock; a zero-copy OpenGL-to-Direct3D bridge that
        bypasses Unity's OpenXR path; a 5G-capable Meta Quest~3; and an
        impairment chain cleared of six transport confounds that each
        mimicked a property of \vpcc over \quic (Section~\ref{sec:system}).
  \item[--] \textbf{An encoding campaign} that characterizes the
        rate--distortion behavior of random-access and low-delay
        configurations for three \gls{mpeg} test sequences and selects
        bitrate-matched operating points (Section~\ref{sec:sys-content}).
  \item[--] \textbf{\crystal}, a mechanistic model whose transport layer
        is parameter-free and whose perceptual layer has one constant,
        replacing per-quality-level regression coefficients with a
        single explanatory variable, $\tlf$ (Section~\ref{sec:model}).
  \item[--] \textbf{A subjective study designed to falsify the model},
        with predictions stated before data collection: that ratings
        from loss-, delay- and interaction-induced misses fall on one
        curve in $\tlf$; that a delay and a loss level that are each
        null alone produce a graded effect together, which no separable
        model in delay and loss can reproduce; and that a single
        $\alpha$ predicts held-out conditions
        (Sections~\ref{sec:design} and~\ref{sec:results}). Every rating
        is aligned with the per-\gof transport log of its session.
\end{itemize}
