\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

The trajectory from \fiveg through 5G-Advanced to \gls{6g} is increasingly
defined by immersive applications~\cite{liberg2024,shen2023toward}:
3GPP and ITU-R list immersive and extended-reality services among the
primary usage scenarios of these generations~\cite{3gpp22261,itur-m2160},
and mobile video traffic, already 76\% of a monthly volume past
\SI{200}{EB}, is projected to more than double by
2031~\cite{mobility25nov}. Each previous generation expanded not only
data rates but the complexity of the media it carried, from analogue
voice in 1G to the ubiquitous 2D video of 4G; the media class that
motivates the latency, throughput and reliability targets of 6G is
immersive media streaming for \gls{vr}: volumetric content captured
from a real scene, transmitted, and reproduced in a fully occlusive
\gls{hmd} with \gls{6dof}, for telepresence, remote collaboration,
education and training~\cite{Immersivevideo,Viola2023}.

The volumetric format at the centre of these applications is the
dynamic point cloud: a sequence of unordered 3D points with colour
attributes, requiring gigabits per second
uncompressed~\cite{pcstreaming21}.
Practical delivery depends on compression, and the \gls{mpeg}
\gls{vpcc} codec, a major component of the \gls{v3c} standard ISO/IEC
23090-5~\cite{v3c,vpccgraziosi}, is the reference choice for dense,
dynamic content: it represents the point cloud as geometry, attribute
and occupancy 2D atlases coded with conventional video codecs such as
\gls{hevc}~\cite{hevc}, and so inherits hardware acceleration and decades of
video-codec engineering. It also inherits the video codec's temporal
prediction structure. A \gls{vpcc} bitstream is organised in \glspl{gof},
and a \gls{gof} is the unit that must be received in full
before any of its frames can be decoded and rendered. Because current
standalone \glspl{hmd} cannot decode \gls{vpcc} in real
time, practical deployments render at an edge server near the radio
access network and cast the rendered stereo frames to the headset;
the transport path between the content origin and the edge renderer is
where network impairment reaches the content.

\paragraph{The transport reality.}
Volumetric streaming research has largely assumed either lossy
datagram transport, in which packet loss manifests as missing
geometry, or research variants of \quic with partial reliability, in
which stale data can be deliberately dropped~\cite{ravuri2023,haseeb2025}.
Neither is what production systems run. The \quic that ships in
browsers, content delivery networks and mobile operating systems is
fully reliable~\cite{rfc9000}: every lost packet is detected,
retransmitted and delivered in order. Under this transport no
bitstream ever reaches the decoder incomplete. Loss is converted into
delay by retransmission and congestion-window reduction, and that
delay is amplified by the \gof structure, since one late packet stalls
the entire \gof it belongs to. The authors' earlier measurements
showed the two impairments to have distinct transport signatures,
added latency shifting the whole delivery-time distribution while loss
expands its tail~\cite{vquest2026}, and showed on \vpcc that a slice
with \emph{lower} loss but a tighter \gof deadline budget scores lower
MOS than a slice with higher loss~\cite{mosaic2026}. The perceptual
consequence of loss under reliable transport is therefore temporal,
not geometric: the viewer sees freezes and frame skips, not holes in
the surface. The quality-of-experience (\qoe) literature has not
modelled this regime.

\paragraph{The modelling gap.}
Network-aware \qoe models for volumetric media exist, but they are
empirical. The closest, by Udora et al.~\cite{qoeholographic,
udora2025thesis}, fits an exponential-in-delay,
linear-in-loss regression with five coefficients per quality level to
subjective scores collected on reliable transport, and reports held-out
correlations of only 0.57--0.66. The model describes the data it was
fitted to; it does not say \emph{why} delay and loss degrade \qoe, and
so gives no principled route to a new encoder configuration, a new
\gof size or a new network. Subjective studies of \vpcc, for their
part, inject loss into bitstreams offline, replay simulated traces, or
assess on 2D displays or \gls{ar} pass-through rather than in fully occlusive
\gls{vr}~\cite{dumic2023,nguyen2023,Cox2023}; streaming systems for \vpcc
report objective metrics and hold the encoder configuration
fixed~\cite{rudolph2025rabbit,qv424} (Section~\ref{sec:related}).

\paragraph{This article.}
We present \crystal, a mechanistic two-layer \qoe model for \vpcc
streaming over reliable \quic. Layer~1 is a transport model: it derives
the \emph{temporal loss fraction} $\tlf$, the fraction of wall-clock
time during which the renderer has no fresh \gof to show, from
measurable transport quantities (delivery slack, round-trip time,
loss-recovery cost and \gof size) and contains no perceptual free
parameter. Layer~2 is a perceptual model with a single parameter:

where $\qc$ is the per-content clean-reference ceiling measured directly
from impairment-free ratings and $\alpha$ is the only fitted perceptual
constant. The model makes a strong, falsifiable claim: under reliable
transport, latency and loss act on \qoe \emph{exclusively} through
$\tlf$, mediated by the codec's delivery-unit structure. We calibrate
and test \crystal with an ITU-T P.910 Absolute Category Rating study
($N=50$) on an end-to-end private \fiveg testbed that delivers \vpcc
content over \gls{http3} to an edge renderer under controlled impairment
and casts the rendered frames to Meta Quest~3 headsets connected
directly to the \fiveg network, using a $2\times2$ factorial design
that isolates latency, loss and their interaction at a fixed encoder
configuration, the decoupling that the three-tier design
of~\cite{mosaic2026} could not provide.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{A mechanistic model.} A two-layer \qoe model for \vpcc
        over reliable \quic in which the transport layer is
        parameter-free and the perceptual layer has one constant,
        replacing five empirically fitted coefficients per quality
        level with a single explanatory variable, $\tlf$.
  \item \textbf{A pre-registered factorial evaluation of the model's
        predictions.} Latency and loss are shown to interact rather
        than add ($1.61\times$), refuting the separability assumed by
        prior regressions; delivery-unit structure rather than loss
        magnitude is shown to govern resilience, with the
        counter-intuitive finding that a larger low-delay \gof is more
        robust than a smaller one; and a single $\alpha$ \todo{is shown
        to / fails to} predict held-out conditions without refitting.
  \item \textbf{A loss-to-latency characterisation of reliable \quic
        for \vpcc.} Per-\gof measurements of stall and deadline-miss
        distributions under controlled impairment on a private \fiveg
        testbed, relating packet loss, delay and \gof structure to
        $\tlf$.
  \item \textbf{A subjective dataset} of \todo{$N$} ratings over three
        \gls{mpeg} CTC sequences and four impairment slices, collected with a
        reproducible Unity-based rating application on the Meta
        Quest~3.
\end{enumerate}
